Lisp vs Scheme

History

Lisp

Lisp was created in 1958 by John McCarthy at MIT, making it one of the oldest high-level programming languages still in use today.

;; Lisp, 1958
(print "hello")

Scheme

Scheme was created in 1975 by Guy L. Steele and Gerald Jay Sussman at MIT as a minimalist, lexically scoped dialect of Lisp.

;; Scheme, 1975
(display "hello")

Syntax

Lisp

(format t "Hello, ~a!~%" "world")

Scheme

(display "Hello, world!")
(newline)

Scoping

Lisp

Common Lisp traditionally supports dynamic scoping for special variables, in addition to lexical scoping for ordinary bindings.

(defvar *x* 10)
(defun show-x () (print *x*))
(let ((*x* 20)) (show-x)) ;; dynamic scope -> 20

Scheme

Scheme uses strict lexical scoping; a variable's binding is determined by where it is defined, not where it is called.

(define x 10)
(define (show-x) (display x))
(let ((x 20)) (show-x)) ;; lexical scope -> 10

Macros

Lisp

(defmacro my-if (test then else)
  `(cond (,test ,then)
         (t ,else)))

Scheme

(define-syntax my-if
  (syntax-rules ()
    ((_ test then else)
     (cond (test then)
           (else else)))))

Standardization

Lisp

Common Lisp was standardized as ANSI INCITS 226-1994 (ANSI Common Lisp), providing a single, stable specification for implementers.

;; ANSI INCITS 226-1994
(defpackage :my-pkg)

Scheme

Scheme is standardized through a series of Revised Reports on the Algorithmic Language Scheme (RnRS), such as R5RS and R7RS.

;; R7RS
(import (scheme base))
